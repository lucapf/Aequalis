Accepted Syntax

One of the values Aequalis aims to bring is understanding what you write the way you would write it. That is evident in the command line, the TUI, the widget and the apps.

Aequalis parses the input as you write it and shows the result.

For example: typing: 2026/12/04 shows: 42.2083 Adding +3d (2026/12/04 +3d) Aequalis 'understands' you want to add 3 days to the date and shows 2026/12/07

There are no strict rules:

  • Case does not matter: usd, USD and Usd are the same, so are new york and NEW YORK, sqrt and SQRT, to and TO. The one exception is where the case tells two things apart: the data units (MB is megabytes, Mb megabits). Written in a case that fits both (mb), Aequalis asks which one you mean.
  • The unit may touch the number: 12usd, 5KM, 100km/h, 1e3km, (2+3)km, 18E6unix. The arrow too: 12USD->EUR.
  • to, in and -> may stand between the two units, places or currencies, or be left out.
  • Numbers as the device writes them are read back: where the thousands separator is ,, 1,000km mi is 1000 km.

Currencies

One amount and one or two currencies. A currency can be written as its code (usd), its name in the singular or plural (euro, US dollars, yen), a symbol (€, £, ¥), a common abbreviation (Dhs, RMB, kr), a country (USA, UAE, China) or Europe / EU. Case and accents don't matter, and the amount may be attached (12usd, 12€, US$12, 12€gbp); to, in or -> may stand between the currencies (12 usd -> eur). Rates: ExchangeRate-API, synced once a day in the background (aequalis --list lists the currencies); a date gives the rates of a past day (below). The amounts below change with the rates.

Rates of a past day

A date at the end of the request (or at its start) gives the rates of that day, up to 750 days back: 100 usd eur 2025-01-30, 100 usd to eur on 30 Jan 2025, 100 usd eur yesterday, 100 usd eur 3 months ago, or short: 12 eur -1d, 12 eur 3 d ago (3d ago, 3 dago). The units are d days, wd working days (Monday to Friday), w weeks, m or mo months, y years. Without a date it is always the latest rates, and nothing is asked. Rates of past days are ExchangeRate-API's, the same as the latest ones, saved once a day. The history starts the day it was set up and grows from then on (there are no rates of earlier days), and the note says which day they are of. A day is downloaded the first time it is asked for and kept on the device, so it works offline afterwards.

The rates.history setting (on by default; the first of the Settings window) turns it on and off, and shows how many days are held (on · 42 days, since 14 Sep 26; also in aequalis --online, About and the apps' settings). On, each rates sync (once a day, in the background) downloads the days not held yet, up to 750; off, nothing is downloaded and a dated request says the history is off (the days already held stay). Offline (the offline setting), only the days already held are available. The files come from rates.history_url, a setting for mirrors.

Calendar

cal (or calendar) shows this month: its weeks with their ISO numbers, today marked, the weekend dimmed. A month's name first needs no cal: oct egypt, march 2027 (a date stays a date: jan 30 2025). After it, a month, a year, and up to 5 countries whose public holidays it marks, each in its colour, separated by commas or spaces (names, codes or the usual short names: UAE, USA, UK). With no country named, the holidays of holidays.countries (the time zone's country by default). A month has its holidays listed under it, in the device's language when the archive has it (else in English), a holiday of several days once with its days together (2-3 Dec AE National Day (2 days)); a year marks them only. The week starts as the device's region does (Monday in Italy, Sunday in the US, Saturday in the UAE), or as calendar.week_start says.

In the interactive mode Enter opens the calendar in a window (← → the month or year before and after, ↑↓ scroll a year; Esc closes it), and C in the Date & Time keys opens this month's; on Android it takes the result's place (a swipe left or right: the month or year after or before; a tap on a holiday shows each country's holiday and how many days it lasts; touching it folds the keyboard away), and the Date & Time panel has a Calendar button. At a terminal the command line colours today and the holidays.

The holidays are every country's national public holidays of last year, this year and next year, from python-holidays (Islamic and other lunar holidays are its estimates, and say so). They are downloaded with the sync and checked once a day (holidays.refresh, holidays.interval), and work offline from then on; before the first sync there are none. The holidays setting (on by default) turns them off; holidays.url is for mirrors.

Distances

Two places, then optionally the unit: [distance] <place> [to] <place> [<unit>]. A place is a city, a country or coordinates (41.9,12.5). With two places the unit is the distance.unit setting (auto: miles where the device is imperial, km elsewhere). to, -> or nothing between the places, in any case.

The world's capitals and the countries are built in: Rome to Paris, ROME PARIS and Rome -> Paris give 1,107 km with no download. With the city archive (aequalis --sync, from GeoNames) every city of 5,000 inhabitants or more is known, by any of its names in any language (Roma, Рим); Springfield, Illinois or Springfield, US picks one. Several cities with that name: Aequalis asks, the country's capital first and selected by default, wherever the device is (Rome: Rome, Italy), then those named exactly so in the device's country, the others there, then the rest, by population. A word that is a unit, a currency or a date keyword keeps that meaning (Bar is a unit: write Bar, Montenegro).
